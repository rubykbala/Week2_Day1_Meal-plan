# 7-Day Vegetarian South Indian Meal Plan Template

**For:** Adults in Tamil Nadu seeking sustainable weight management  
**Use:** A reusable starting template. Portions and calorie values are estimates; adjust to the individual with a qualified dietitian where needed.

This plan uses familiar foods and a regular four-meal structure. It is not a fixed-calorie prescription. “VLCC-style” refers only to a clear, portion-led plan format; no proprietary plan or material is reproduced.

## Practical customization notes

- **Build a satisfying plate:** Include vegetables at lunch and dinner, a protein-rich food at each main meal where possible, and a measured grain portion. Useful vegetarian proteins include dal, sambar with adequate dal, beans, chickpeas, green gram, peas, tofu, paneer, curd, and milk.
- **Adjust portions to the person:** Increase or reduce grain portions modestly according to hunger, activity, body-size goals, and progress. Keep protein and vegetables adequate rather than cutting meals sharply. Persistent hunger, fatigue, or declining exercise capacity are signs to reassess the plan.
- **Oil and extras:** Measure oil rather than pouring freely. Chutney, coconut, ghee, fried toppings, sweetened drinks, and second helpings can add meaningful calories; they can still fit in planned portions.
- **Flexible swaps (roughly similar meal role, not exact calorie matches):**
  - 2 idlis ↔ 1 medium dosa ↔ 2 small adai/uthappam, adjusting chutney/oil and sambar portions.
  - ¾–1 cup cooked rice ↔ 2 medium phulkas ↔ about 1 cup cooked millet. Grain calories differ by recipe and cooked weight.
  - ¾ cup cooked beans/channa/sundal ↔ about 100 g tofu ↔ 75–100 g paneer or 1 cup thick dal/kootu. Paneer is usually more energy-dense; choose portions accordingly.
  - One seasonal fruit ↔ one similar-sized fruit serving. Prefer whole fruit to juice.
  - Choose local seasonal vegetables for poriyal, kootu, avial, or sambar; use modest oil and vary colors and types.
- **For reuse with clients:** Keep the dish pattern and replace foods based on preference, budget, season, allergies, culture, and access. Record the client's usual portions and activity before adjusting. These estimates are not a substitute for recipe-specific calculation.

## Hydration and activity

Drink water regularly across the day and use thirst, climate, and activity to guide intake. Carry water in hot weather; do not force a fixed volume if a clinician has prescribed fluid limits. Unsweetened water, tea, or coffee can be included; limit sugary drinks.

Aim for regular movement that is appropriate to fitness and health, such as brisk walking and enjoyable daily activity. Add strength-building activity on a regular basis if safe and feasible, and increase activity gradually. Follow local clinical advice if exercise is restricted.

## Important disclaimer

This is general educational meal-planning information, not individualized medical advice or a guaranteed weight-loss prescription. Calorie needs vary by **age, sex, body size, activity level, and health conditions**; food needs can also change with pregnancy, breastfeeding, medication, and recovery from illness. A qualified dietitian or clinician should personalize portions and calorie targets, especially for diabetes, kidney or heart disease, gastrointestinal conditions, eating-disorder history, pregnancy, breastfeeding, or other medical needs. Avoid extreme restriction and seek professional guidance for persistent symptoms or unintended weight change.
