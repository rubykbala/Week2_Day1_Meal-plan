# 7-Day Nut-Aware, Lower-Sodium South Indian Meal Plan Template

**For:** Adults in Tamil Nadu with a nut allergy and high blood pressure (hypertension)  
**Format:** Four meals a day; portions and calories are approximate planning estimates.

This is a practical, reusable template, not a fixed-calorie or medical prescription. “Nut-aware” means the listed recipes do not intentionally use peanuts or tree nuts. It does **not** guarantee allergen-free ingredients or preparation. Confirm the client's exact allergy list (including whether peanuts, particular tree nuts, seeds, or coconut must be avoided) with the client and their allergy clinician before use.

## Nut-allergy safety

- Confirm exactly which nuts and related foods the client must avoid. “Nut allergy” can mean different things; do not assume a food is safe based on the name alone. If the client has been advised to avoid peanuts, tree nuts, seeds, coconut, or particular oils, follow that specific advice.
- Check the full ingredient and allergen statements every time for packaged foods such as oats, rava, semiya, millet, paneer, curd, spices, chutneys, flour, and roasted chana. Recipes and manufacturing can change. Avoid products that list the client's allergen and follow the client's clinician's advice about precautionary “may contain” or shared-facility statements.
- Ask about restaurant, bakery, mill, and caterer preparation. Shared chutney grinders, oil, fryers, pans, ladles, counters, storage bins, and serving spoons can cause cross-contact. If safe preparation cannot be confirmed, don't serve that item.
- Before cooking, wash hands and clean counters, boards, knives, pans, and utensils thoroughly with soap and water. Keep the client's ingredients covered and separate; prepare and serve the allergy-safe meal with clean utensils. Simply picking nuts out of food does not make it safe.
- This template cannot guarantee safety from cross-contact. Follow the client's allergy action plan and clinician's instructions; keep prescribed emergency medication available as directed.

## Lower-sodium preparation for blood pressure

- Build flavor with lemon, tamarind, curry leaves, garlic, ginger, pepper, cumin, coriander, mustard seed, turmeric, chilli, and fresh herbs rather than relying on extra salt.
- Limit added salt and taste before adding more. Avoid or minimize pickle, papad/appalam, salted snacks, instant mixes, stock cubes, packaged soups, instant noodles, salty sauces, and heavily salted spice mixes. Choose plain, fresh ingredients where possible and compare package sodium labels.
- Rinse canned beans if used, or use home-cooked pulses without added salt. Avoid salty packaged roasted chana; use fresh homemade or clearly labeled unsalted options, checking allergen statements.
- Do not use potassium-based salt substitutes unless the client's clinician approves them. They may be unsafe with some kidney conditions or medicines.
- Individual sodium goals should be set with the treating clinician or dietitian, especially with kidney disease, heart failure, pregnancy, or blood-pressure medicines. WHO recommends adults generally consume less than 2,000 mg sodium per day (under 5 g salt), but this is general guidance and may not be the client's prescribed target. [WHO sodium guidance](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/sodium-reduction)

## Portion swaps and routine

- For a grain serving, use approximately ¾–1 cup cooked rice or millet, 2 medium phulkas, or 2 medium dosas. These are approximate swaps; recipes and sizes differ.
- For a protein serving, rotate dal, sambar with a reasonable amount of dal, chickpeas, green gram, other beans, tofu, paneer, and plain curd. Choose allergy-safe products and check labels.
- Swap fruit with a similar whole-fruit serving; prefer whole fruit over juice. Use seasonal vegetables in poriyal, kootu, avial, and sambar.
- Keep oil measured and moderate. If a client remains hungry or feels weak, reassess the portions and overall energy intake rather than insisting on restriction.
- Drink water regularly, guided by thirst, climate, and activity; follow medical fluid limits if prescribed. Choose unsweetened drinks most often.
- Encourage regular enjoyable movement, such as walking and daily activity, with strength-building activity when appropriate. Increase gradually and follow clinician advice about exercise limits.

## Important disclaimer

This is general educational meal-planning information, not individualized medical advice, allergy clearance, or a treatment plan for hypertension. Calorie and sodium needs vary, and food choices may need to change with age, sex, body size, activity, health conditions, pregnancy or breastfeeding, and medications. A qualified dietitian or clinician should personalize this template, particularly for uncontrolled high blood pressure, kidney or heart disease, diabetes, medication interactions, or complex allergies. Continue prescribed medicines and clinical follow-up; do not make medication changes based on this meal plan.

Allergy-preparation guidance: [Food Allergy Research & Education — Avoiding Cross-Contact](https://www.foodallergy.org/resources/avoiding-cross-contact).
